# Format: [Title]|[Content]

[Sun]|The Sun is a star at the center of our solar system. It provides light and heat, making life on Earth possible. It is mostly made of hydrogen and helium.

[Moon]|The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It affects tides and has phases like full moon and new moon. Astronauts landed on it in 1969 during the Apollo 11 mission.

[Earth]|Earth is the third planet from the Sun. It has water, air, and land, supporting diverse life forms. It is the only known planet with human life.

[Mars]|Mars is the fourth planet, known as the Red Planet due to its iron-rich soil. It has the largest volcano and canyon in the solar system.

[Jupiter]|Jupiter is the fifth planet and the largest in our solar system. It has a Great Red Spot, a storm larger than Earth, and many moons.

[Saturn]|Saturn is known for its beautiful rings made of ice and dust. It is the sixth planet and has a low density, floating in water if a big enough ocean existed.

[Venus]|Venus is the second planet, similar in size to Earth. It has a thick atmosphere and extreme heat, making it the hottest planet.

[Mercury]|Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun. It has no atmosphere and extreme temperature changes between day and night.

[Uranus]|Uranus is the seventh planet, tilted on its side. It has a faint ring system and a pale blue color due to methane.

[Neptune]|Neptune is the eighth planet, known for its deep blue color and strong winds. It was discovered in 1846.

[Water Cycle]|The water cycle describes how water moves from oceans to air and back. It includes evaporation, condensation, and precipitation.

[Photosynthesis]|Photosynthesis is how plants make food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide. It produces oxygen for us to breathe.

[Rainforest]|Rainforests are dense forests with high rainfall. They are home to many animals and plants, like monkeys and orchids.

[Desert]|Deserts are dry areas with little rain. Animals like camels live there, adapted to store water.

[Ocean]|Oceans cover most of Earth’s surface. They are home to fish, whales, and coral reefs.

[Mountain]|Mountains are high landforms created by tectonic plates. People climb them for adventure.

[River]|Rivers are flowing water bodies that often start in mountains and end in seas.

[Lake]|Lakes are large bodies of water surrounded by land. Some are home to fish and birds.

[Volcano]|Volcanoes are mountains that can erupt with lava. They shape the Earth’s surface.

[Earthquake]|Earthquakes are sudden shakes of the ground caused by moving tectonic plates.

[Animal]|Animals are living creatures that move and eat food. Examples include dogs, birds, and fish.

[Plant]|Plants are living things that make their own food using sunlight. They include trees and flowers.

[Bird]|Birds are animals with feathers and wings. They can fly, like eagles and sparrows.

[Fish]|Fish live in water and breathe with gills. Examples include sharks and goldfish.

[Insect]|Insects have six legs and antennae. Bees and butterflies are common insects.

[Reptile]|Reptiles have scales and lay eggs. Snakes and lizards are examples.

[Mammal]|Mammals have hair and feed milk to their babies. Humans and whales are mammals.

[Dinosaur]|Dinosaurs were large reptiles that lived millions of years ago. Some were as big as houses.

[Space]|Space is the vast area beyond Earth. It contains stars, planets, and galaxies.

[Galaxy]|A galaxy is a huge group of stars. The Milky Way is our galaxy.

[Star]|Stars are huge balls of gas that produce light and heat, like the Sun.

[Planet]|Planets orbit stars. Earth is one of eight planets in our solar system.

[Astronaut]|Astronauts are people trained to travel and work in space.

[Rocket]|Rockets are vehicles that carry people or satellites into space.

[Satellite]|Satellites are objects that orbit Earth, used for weather and communication.

[Gravity]|Gravity is a force that pulls objects toward each other. It keeps us on Earth.

[Energy]|Energy is the ability to do work. It comes from the Sun, food, and electricity.

[Light]|Light is what we see with. It comes from the Sun and lamps.

[Sound]|Sound is made by vibrations. We hear it with our ears.

[Weather]|Weather includes rain, snow, and sunshine. It changes every day.

[Cloud]|Clouds are water droplets in the sky. They bring rain or snow.

[Wind]|Wind is moving air. It can be gentle or strong like a storm.

[Snow]|Snow is frozen water that falls from clouds in cold weather.

[Rain]|Rain is water that falls from clouds. It helps plants grow.

[Storm]|Storms are strong weather with rain, wind, and sometimes thunder.

[Thunder]|Thunder is the sound made by lightning. It can be loud and scary.

[Lightning]|Lightning is a bright flash of electricity during a storm.

[Forest]|Forests are areas with many trees. They are homes for animals.

[Grassland]|Grasslands have lots of grass and few trees. Zebras live there.

[Tundra]|Tundra is a cold, treeless area. It has moss and lichens.

[Coral Reef]|Coral reefs are underwater structures made by tiny animals called corals.

[Whale]|Whales are large sea mammals that sing and migrate long distances.

[Shark]|Sharks are fish with sharp teeth. They live in oceans.

[Dolphin]|Dolphins are smart mammals that live in the sea and love to play.

[Turtle]|Turtles are reptiles with hard shells. Some live on land, some in water.

[Frog]|Frogs are amphibians that live near water. They start as tadpoles.

[Butterfly]|Butterflies are insects with colorful wings. They start as caterpillars.

[Bee]|Bees make honey and help flowers grow by pollination.

[Ant]|Ants are small insects that live in colonies and work together.

[Spider]|Spiders are arachnids with eight legs. They make webs to catch food.

[Elephant]|Elephants are large mammals with trunks. They live in Africa and Asia.
